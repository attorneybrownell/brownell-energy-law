PPA Review — Meridian Ridge Solar

To: Northline Renewables Partners LLC (Manager) · From: Brownell Energy Law · Re: Buyer draft Power Purchase Agreement dated July 14, 2026 (62 pp.) · Demonstration — all parties, facts, and documents are fictional

Executive summary

Granite Peak's draft is a buyer's form with three problems that matter more than everything else combined: a termination-for-convenience right priced far below your actual loss, an assignment clause that would block your financing outright, and a 25-year revenue stream resting on an unrated shell counterparty with no parent support. None of these is unusual in a first draft, and all three are fixable — but the deal is not financeable, and should not be signed, until they are. Beyond the top three, seven further issues need attention; most are winnable with standard asks.

Bottom line: negotiate before you sign, in the order set out below. With the recommended package accepted, this is a good contract: the price and term are sound, and the delivery mechanics are conventional.

Issues, ranked by risk

Critical

1 · Termination for convenience without a make-whole

§ 11.2
What the draft says
Buyer may terminate at any time on 180 days' notice, paying only the "unamortized capital cost" of the facility.
Why it matters
The payment ignores your lost margin, lost tax credits and recapture exposure, and financing breakage. A termination in year 6 would repay the steel but strand the economics — and no tax equity investor will close against a revenue stream the counterparty can extinguish at book value.
Recommended position
Delete the convenience right; if Buyer insists, replace the payment with a scheduled termination value (attached as an exhibit, year by year) covering debt breakage, tax recapture, and lost equity return.
Fallback
Convenience right exercisable only after year 10, at the exhibit termination value, with 12 months' notice.
Critical

2 · Assignment clause blocks financing

§ 14.1
What the draft says
No assignment of any kind — expressly including collateral assignment — without Buyer's consent in its sole discretion.
Why it matters
Every financing you contemplate requires a collateral assignment to lenders and transfers within the tax equity partnership. As written, Buyer holds a veto over your capital structure. This is the single most common financing blocker in offtaker paper, and the single most routinely conceded.
Recommended position
Carve out (a) collateral assignments to financing parties, (b) assignments to affiliates and tax equity vehicles, and (c) assignment to any successor meeting a stated net-worth test — all without consent. Add Buyer's agreement to deliver a standard consent-to-assignment and estoppel to financing parties on request.
Fallback
Consent "not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned, or delayed," with the financing carve-outs preserved verbatim — the carve-outs are the non-negotiable core.
High

3 · No credit support behind a 25-year obligation

§ 16
What the draft says
The contracting Buyer is GPB Operations LLC, an unrated special-purpose subsidiary. No guaranty, letter of credit, or other support is offered.
Why it matters
Your lenders will underwrite the offtaker, not the project. An unrated shell makes the revenue contract worth little more than the shell's balance sheet.
Recommended position
Parent guaranty from Granite Peak Beverage Co. for all payment obligations, or an evergreen letter of credit sized at 12 months of expected revenue.
Fallback
Guaranty capped at 24 months of revenue plus termination value, with a ratings/net-worth trigger requiring an LC if parent credit deteriorates.
High

4 · Uncapped output-shortfall damages

§ 5.2
What the draft says
Seller guarantees 95% of "Expected Annual Output"; shortfalls are paid at 150% of the contract price, without cap and without a degradation allowance.
Why it matters
Solar output varies with weather; panels degrade on a known curve. An uncapped guarantee at a punitive multiple converts ordinary resource variability into open-ended liability.
Recommended position
Guarantee measured over rolling 2-year periods against a P90 estimate net of scheduled degradation (0.5%/yr); shortfall payments at 100% of contract price, capped annually at 10% of that year's expected revenue.
Fallback
Single-year measurement retained, but with the degradation curve, a weather-adjustment mechanism, and the annual cap.
High

5 · COD deadline with day-one delay damages and early termination

§ 3.4
What the draft says
Guaranteed COD 14 months from execution; $500/day delay damages from day one; Buyer may terminate at 90 days late. Force majeure excludes supply-chain disruption and interconnection delays.
Why it matters
The two most common causes of solar schedule slip — equipment delivery and the utility's own interconnection timeline — are precisely the ones carved out of force majeure. You would be strictly liable for delays you cannot control.
Recommended position
18-month guaranteed COD with a 90-day grace period before damages; force majeure to include documented equipment delivery delays and any delay caused by the interconnecting utility; termination right only at 12 months late.
Fallback
Keep 14 months, but with day-for-day extension for utility-caused delay and a 60-day grace period.
High

6 · Purchase option priced by Buyer's own appraiser

§ 12.3
What the draft says
Buyer may purchase the facility at years 7, 10, and 15 at "fair market value determined by an independent appraiser selected by Buyer."
Why it matters
A one-sided appraisal is not fair market value, and a year-7 option needs a floor above the tax equity flip to avoid recapture complications.
Recommended position
First option date no earlier than year 10 (post-flip); FMV by the standard three-appraiser mechanism (each side appoints one, the two select a third, middle value governs); floor at the greater of FMV and the termination value schedule.
Fallback
Year-7 date retained with the appraisal mechanism and floor — the pricing procedure is the part that cannot give.
Medium

7 · Uncompensated, uncapped curtailment

§ 6.1
What the draft says
Buyer may reduce or refuse delivery at any time without payment.
Recommended position
Buyer curtailment limited to 200 hours/year without compensation; beyond that, deemed-generated energy is paid at the contract price. Utility- or emergency-directed curtailment remains uncompensated.
Fallback
400 free hours with the deemed-generation mechanic above the cap.
Medium

8 · One-way indemnity with consequential damages

§ 17.4
What the draft says
Seller indemnifies Buyer including consequential damages; the consequential-damages waiver protects Buyer only.
Recommended position
Mutual indemnities; mutual consequential-damages waiver (delay and shortfall damages under §§ 3.4 and 5.2 excepted as the sole liquidated remedies).
Fallback
None needed — mutuality here is a standard concession.
Medium

9 · Insurance requirements oversized for the asset

§ 9.2
What the draft says
$10M per-occurrence umbrella; Buyer named additional insured on all policies including property.
Recommended position
$5M umbrella (market for this asset size); Buyer as additional insured on liability policies only, with waiver of subrogation; lender-standard notice-of-cancellation language.
Fallback
$10M accepted if Buyer bears the incremental premium above $5M-equivalent cover.
Medium

10 · All change-of-law risk on Seller

§ 21.7
What the draft says
Seller bears every regulatory and tax change, including property tax reassessment, with no price adjustment.
Recommended position
Change-of-law risk shared: price reopener for changes increasing Seller's cost of performance by more than 5%; property taxes addressed through a PILOT or pass-through above a baseline.
Fallback
Seller retains general change-of-law risk but property tax above the year-1 baseline passes through.

Recommended sequence

  1. Lead with Issues 1–3 as a single financeability package — frame them as what any financeable PPA requires, not as commercial asks.
  2. Trade within Issues 4–6: the appraisal-procedure fix (6) and mutual indemnity (8) are cheap for Buyer to give; use them to hold the line on the performance-guarantee cap (4).
  3. Concede insurance limits (9) last, and only against the credit support you need (3).

The full ask/fallback/walk-away matrix is maintained in the negotiation runbook, and the board-facing summary of this memo is the presentation deck.
